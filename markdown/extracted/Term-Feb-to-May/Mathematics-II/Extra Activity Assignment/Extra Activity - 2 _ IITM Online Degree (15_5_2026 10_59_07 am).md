In this activity, we will take a look at the application of geogebra to visualize linear transformations $T : \\mathbb{R}^2 → \\mathbb{R}^2$ . The notation in the questions that follow will be as in the following project  
  
[https://www.geogebra.org/m/krqstvdm](https://www.geogebra.org/m/krqstvdm)  
  
The description of the elements in the project is as follows:  
  
• The parameters $a, b, c$ and $d$ are variables used to determine the linear transformation whose matrix representation with respect to the standard bases is $\\begin{bmatrix}a & b \\\\c & d \\end{bmatrix}$  
  
• $Te1$ and $Te2$ are vectors which represent the images of the standard basis vectors $e1$ and $e2$, respectively.  
  
• u is an arbitrary vector in the plane, and $Tu$ is its image under $T$.  
  
• $P$ is a quadrilateral in the plane and $TP$ is its image under $T$.  
  
• $S$ is a circular sector in the plane and $TS$ represents its image under $T$.  
  
To specify the transformation, one can specify the values of $a, b, c$ and $d$. Or alternatively, move the vectors $Te1$ and $Te2$ to specify the image of the standard basis, thus determining the linear transformation. Once a linear transformation is fixed, the vector u can be moved by dragging its arrowhead. The vector $Tu$ changes accordingly to give the image of the vector $u$. Similarly, the elements $P$ and $S$ can be moved around and $TP$ and $TS$ can be analyzed to understand the geometric properties of the linear transformation. Answer the following questions using the given project.

In the geogebra project, this corresponds to moving the vectors T e1 and T e2 so that the arrowheads are on $(λ, 0)$ and $(0, λ)$, respectively, for some $λ ∈ \\mathbb{R}$ \\ {0} of your choice. Which of the following statements are true for such a linear transformation?

 The matrix of T with respect to the standard bases for the domain and codomain is a scalar matrix.

If λ < 0, the angle between u and T(u) is $180 ^ ◦$ , for all u ∈ $\\mathbb{R} ^ 2$ \\ {(0, 0)}.

 If P is the square with vertices (0, 0),(1, 0),(1, 1) and (0, 1), then its image T P has area equal to λ square units.

 The linear transformation T is always a bijection.

 If λ > 0, then there is a non-zero vector u such that T(u) = u.
